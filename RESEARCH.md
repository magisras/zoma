# Twenty Tons — Research Backbone

Oct 2, 2026 · @Sarutis

## Concept

Twenty Tons is a serious, realistic game about a Dhaka bus crew: you don't play a hero, you play people trapped in a pay system that rewards racing and punishes caution. Like This War of Mine, the point is to let the player feel why the choices get made, not to make the chaos fun.

- Single player (driver with AI crew) or 3-player co-op (driver, helper, conductor) on the same bus.
- Fictional city modelled on Dhaka; real economics; permanent consequences; no respawns.
- Platform plan: Unity, Steam.

## Pay system: the zoma

The owner gets a fixed daily deposit (the *zoma*) no matter what; the crew keeps only what is left after the zoma, fuel and roadside payments. A slow day, a breakdown or an hour of rain is the crew's loss, never the owner's. Crews hand over the money at the end of the shift.

| Item | Figure | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross daily takings (Dhaka city bus) | \~Tk 3,000 | before deductions |
| Extortion stops on one route (Mirpur 12–Azimpur) | 8–10 locations | paid per trip |
| Average monthly earnings, drivers | \~Tk 22,000 | one study: 22,120; a third earn Tk 15,000–20,000 |
| Intercity (Rajshahi–Dhaka) per trip | driver Tk 1,100 / helper Tk 400 | unchanged for \~10 years |
| Child helpers on human-haulers | as little as Tk 250/day | often aged 14+ |

A minimum wage for transport workers exists on paper since 2018, but the deposit and per-trip system sets real pay. In 2018 the owners' association announced monthly salaries for Dhaka drivers; the deposit system continued in practice.

## Crew, training and gap chasing

A city bus runs with three people, and each role is a game role.

| Role | What they do | Why the bus fails without them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Driver | drives, picks when to race, when to stop | the bus moves |
| Helper | hangs from the door, calls stops, pulls passengers aboard, watches the bus behind | without him the bus behind takes your passengers |
| Conductor | collects fares, argues half-fares, tracks the count | without him the money leaks |

**Who they are.** Mostly rural migrants from poor families. Helpers can be 14. Training is informal: a helper watches an *ostad* (senior driver) until someone decides he can drive. Close to 90% of commercial drivers have no institutional training; many hold no valid licence; three in four don't know the speed limits.

**Gap chasing.** Buses of the same company run the same route and compete with each other. If another bus is behind, the helper signals the driver to hurry; when buses bunch up, conductors shout to "mind the gap." If the bus ahead leaves first, your waiting passengers walk to it. Owners say this competition is why buses get battered so fast.

**Waybill.** Owners used inspectors at points along the route who logged departure time and passenger count; pay was computed from passengers times fare. Dhaka owners scrapped waybill checks in August 2022 after fare disputes. Experts have long proposed one company per route to end the racing.

Sources: [Daily Star, Aug 2026](https://www.thedailystar.net/slow-reads/big-picture/news/why-blaming-dhakas-bus-drivers-misses-the-real-problem-4244066), [bdnews24, 2024](https://bdnews24.com/bangladesh/a01a0cb5c7f7), [Dhaka Tribune, 2022](https://www.dhakatribune.com/dhaka/2022/08/11/transport-owners-announce-cancellation-of-bus-waybill-check)

## Sleep, shifts and health

The common rhythm is one day on, one day off: a crew survives two days on one day's income. Shifts stretch to 17 hours; terminal surveys find 12 to 14 hours of driving is routine. No sick pay, so resting means not eating.

| Finding | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Average working day (3-city study) | 9.25 h; 62% work 8–14 h |
| Sleep disturbances | 46.6% of drivers |
| Low back pain | 66.5% (three-city study); 34.5% in the past month (Dhaka terminals) |
| Muscular pain | 58.7% |
| Fatigue-related fatal accidents (police, 2010) | 1,911 of 2,437 reported accidents |
| Drivers with fatigue-related disability or psycho-social disorders | \~83% |

Many crews sleep inside parked buses or on terminal floors between shifts, with no wash and no proper night's sleep. In-cabin noise often reaches hearing-damaging levels; headaches, fatigue and raised blood pressure follow. Seven to nine hours of sleep on the off day measurably reduces traffic violations.

## Bus costs, maintenance and fitness

The regulator's fare model assumes a well-kept bus; the street runs on worn ones.

| Item | Official assumption (BRTA cost model) | Reality reported |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New metropolitan bus | Tk 3.5 million, 10-year life | buses of that value are rarely seen in Dhaka |
| Renovation | Tk 650,000 every 5 years | — |
| Maintenance | every 25 days and every 3 months, \~Tk 40,000 | little evidence it happens |
| Garage and terminal | Tk 65,000 | most buses park on roadsides |
| New 40-seater (Tata, Bangladesh list price) | Tk 5–7 million | explains why owners run old stock |

Fitness certificates are unreliable: owners have admitted getting certificates for buses never inspected, part of a wider extortion-and-collusion pattern documented by Transparency International Bangladesh. Drivers say constant racing is why buses become battered quickly. Dhaka has roughly 52,000 private buses nationwide against about 1,600 state (BRTC) buses.

## Accidents and consequences

Buses are involved in about one in four Dhaka crashes, and the crew is the first to pay.

| Measure (2025) | Figure | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crashes / deaths / injuries, Bangladesh | 7,584 / 7,359 / 16,476 | Road Safety Foundation |
| Crashes / deaths in Dhaka city | 409 / 219 | Road Safety Foundation |
| Share of Dhaka crashes involving buses | 24.87% | Road Safety Foundation |
| Share of national crashes involving buses | 13.9–14.5% | RSF; Passenger Welfare Association |
| Transport workers killed on the road | 1,776 | Passenger Welfare Association |
| Dhaka hotspots | Jatrabari, Demra, Mohammadpur, Kuril Bishwa Road, Airport Road | Road Safety Foundation |

**What follows a crash.** The driver may face an angry crowd, even assault, before fault is known. The 2018 Road Transport Act (passed after two students were killed by a racing bus) set heavy, non-bailable penalties for causing death; owner syndicates lobbied to soften them within a year. Passengers describe rollovers where the driver fled on the spot. There is no pension and no compensation when injury ends a career; one illness can put a household in debt for years.

## Game mechanics from the research

The appeal is the trap: the player understands every bad decision because the system made it the sensible one.

**The daily ledger (core loop).** Each shift ends with one equation: fares collected minus zoma minus fuel minus extortion stops equals what the crew eats. The zoma never moves. Rain, jams and breakdowns come out of the crew's pocket.

**Gap chasing (moment-to-moment tension).** AI buses of your own company run ahead and behind. The helper reports the gap; too close behind means losing your stop, too far ahead means an empty bus. Arriving second at a stop earns almost nothing.

**Three roles that need each other.**

| Role | Player verbs | Failure if missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Driver | speed, lane, when to stop, when to skip | nothing moves |
| Helper | call stops, grab passengers mid-roll, watch the gap, close doors | bus behind steals the stop; people fall at the door |
| Conductor | collect fares, argue half-fares, count, hide cash at extortion points | money leaks; disputes slow the bus |

Solo play gives the driver AI crew; co-op puts three players on one bus.

**Fatigue.** A 12–17 hour shift with a one-on, one-off rhythm. Reaction time slows, vision tunnels, micro-sleeps close the screen for a moment. Sleeping in the bus recovers less than a bed, and the bed costs money.

**The bus as a character.** A 20-ton vehicle with worn brakes and heavy steering. Repairs cost real money you don't have; skipping them is a loan against tomorrow. The fitness certificate can be bought and means nothing.

**Consequences that stay.** A crash can injure crew or passengers, bring a crowd, an arrest, an impounded bus, a family without income. No respawns. Between shifts: short scenes of calls home, debt, the terminal floor.

**The rollover (from the Fraser video).** A driver dozes off, drifts into the railing, and the bus tips onto its side with everyone inside. Locals arrive with ropes and a winch, drag it back onto its wheels, and the bus goes straight back into service. In the game this becomes a set piece with a choice attached: micro-sleep → tip → passengers and crew tumble → crowd gathers → someone offers ropes and a tractor for a price → you pay, lose the rest of the day's takings, and still owe the zoma, or you walk away from the bus. The bus keeps dents, a cracked windscreen and a bent door afterwards; the crew carries injuries into the next shift. No cinematic, no reward: it is just what happens when the fatigue meter is ignored.

**Bribes and extortion.** Three payers along the route, all from the day's takings before the owner's deposit: the lineman at the terminal (flat, unavoidable; the association's "fees"), the sergeant at a junction (negotiable: pay now or take a case that costs more and a seized bus), and the party man at a stand (varies with who is in power). A fit bus with clean papers lowers how often the sergeant can stop you and how expensive a stop gets, but never removes the lineman or the party man. Payments disguised as night guard, cleaner or lineman wages; cash or bKash. Nationally, at least Tk 1,059 crore a year (TIB 2024); in Dhaka \~Tk 2.21 crore a day across 53 terminals and stands (2025 probe). 29% of workers say they have bribed traffic or highway police; 28% have had cases filed against them. About 30% of Dhaka's buses are past legal life yet run. [TIB via Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/transport/news/bus-operators-pay-tk-1059cr-year-bribes-3559756), [Daily Star 2025](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/transport/news/tk-221cr-extorted-daily-city-transport-3842886), [Prothom Alo 2026](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/0zvmbfozgo)

**Contact between buses and traffic rules.** Buses touch constantly; dents and scraped paint are the norm, and racing buses have crushed people between them (Bangla Motor, 2018). Drivers stop centimetres short because damage comes out of the crew's day, a stuck bus is lost trips, and a hit can turn into a crew brawl. In the game: light contact is common and cosmetic (paint, mirror, a shouting match that costs a minute); harder contact triggers a crew confrontation settled on the spot (pay a little, argue, or lose time; both sides want it over, nobody calls police because both buses would be seized); only injuries escalate to a crowd, police, a criminal case and the end of the day. Insurance is effectively absent. There is no "report your rival" option; blocking and cutting in are how the job works, and rules are enforced selectively (obstruction, licence, permit, wrong side). Caveat: on-the-spot settlement is inferred from how the sector works; confirm from ride-along footage.

**No villain on the road.** Rival drivers are in the same trap. The antagonists are the deposit system, the owners and the missing bus stops.

**Tone.** Realistic, not comic. Fictional city built from real Dhaka geography and economics; consult Bangladeshi crews and a road-safety organisation; consider supporting a real cause.

## Driving culture (the unwritten rules)

Almost nobody follows the written code; almost everybody follows the unwritten one. The game models the second.

- **Size is right of way.** Buses and trucks dominate; cars, CNGs and rickshaws yield or get pushed. A bus yields only to a flagged car or a sergeant. Pedestrians step into traffic with one hand raised to stop it.
- **Space belongs to whoever takes it.** Lanes are decorative; nobody queues; drivers force in from the sides and overtake on the left.
- **The horn is language.** "I'm here", "coming through", "move"; continuous. A quiet bus has lost its nerve.
- **Hazard lights mean straight ahead.** Right for right, left for left, hazards for straight.
- **The police hand is the traffic light.** Signals are ignored; officers with a cane (sometimes a rope) release lanes; drivers keep crossing red until the opposing flow physically stops them.
- **Wrong side is normal** to beat jams, often at passengers' urging; even ministers' cars do it.
- **Night is dark.** Many drive without lights after sundown.
- **Passengers push the driver**: faster, stop here, take the wrong side.
- **The one hard rule: don't hit a person.** Injuring a pedestrian brings the crowd and the law.
- **Changing:** since April 2026 AI traffic cameras fine owners by text (\~Tk 2,000 for a red light); coverage partial. Story hook: cameras appearing along the route over time.
- **Context:** average Dhaka speed fell from \~21 km/h (2006) to \~5 km/h (2024); racing happens in bursts between walls of jam.

**Teaching the player the rules of chaos**

- *Act 1 as helper is the tutorial.* The ostad drives; the player watches the hierarchy, the horn, the hand, and learns what a near miss looks like before being responsible for one.
- *The world enforces, not the UI.* No tutorial pop-ups. Follow Western lane discipline and the bus behind takes your stop, rickshaws fill every gap you leave, and the ledger comes up short. Push, honk and claim space and the road opens. Drive quietly and it closes.
- *Horn as a mechanic.* A horn button with real meaning: short taps clear a path, a long blast warns the rickshaw ahead; no horn and smaller vehicles don't move. Passengers and the helper react to silence ("why are you not honking?").
- *The crew teaches.* The helper shouts the gap, the conductor tells you to take the wrong side, passengers shout to go faster; their voices are the manual.
- *Hand signals work.* The officer's cane matters; red lights don't. The player learns to read bodies, not lights.
- *Hitting a person is the only real punishment.* Scrapes are cosmetic, cutting in is normal, but a pedestrian contact triggers the crowd, the case and the end of the day. The game's one hard rule is the street's one hard rule.
- *A mirror moment.* Early on, let the player drive "properly" once and watch the whole shift fail. They should understand, not be told, why nobody drives like that.

## Passengers, stops and police

**No real stops.** Passengers get down almost anywhere and buses pick up at any point, so the whole road is a bus stop. At junctions buses stop mid-road when someone waves; drivers pull ahead of rival buses and park diagonally across lanes to grab a crowd. Most helpers don't know where the official stops are. Hotspots: Farmgate, Karwan Bazar (buses queue and load mid-road), Azimpur and Nilkhet (no bays, stop at the intersection), Mohakhali, Uttara/Abdullahpur.

**The full/empty dial.** A bus with its seats full skips the next stops; a bus below its threshold waits longer and fishes. Drivers deliberately wait at the early stops to fill up; about 70% of all waiting happens before the halfway mark, near the high-demand origin. Waiting is longer on weekends when demand is low.

**Boarding time.** 2 to 6 seconds per passenger, more with pushing. The conductor collects fares after departure so the door isn't blocked. Slow cases: elderly, luggage and sacks, children, fare arguments (student half-fare is the classic fight), people standing in the doorway.

**On and off.** Helpers mostly shout, grab and pull rather than push; picking up without stopping is common. Alighting is the dangerous part: passengers are forced off running buses. Flow: residential ends (Mirpur, Uttara, Gazipur) fill the bus in the morning; the commercial core (Farmgate, Shahbagh, Paltan, Motijheel, Gulistan) empties and reloads it; evening reverses. Metro has cut bus ridership on the Mirpur–Motijheel corridor by up to 30%; owners say daily ticket sales per bus fell from Tk 6,000–6,500 to Tk 4,000–5,500 (2024). Local buses run day and night; "sitting service" runs daytime and usually just charges a flat fare for standing.

**Police.** DMP Traffic has \~3,900 officers against 4,200 posts: constables at junctions, sergeants in the field filing cases. Bus companies say they are the main target (obstruction, licence, route permit). Wrong-side driving: Tk 3,000 per case. Sergeants carry body cameras. Separately, crews pay cash at 8–10 points along a route. The 2018 protests were the only period in memory when roads functioned lawfully.

**For the game:** demand zones along the road rather than fixed stops, with hot clusters at junctions; per-passenger boarding times; a sergeant who can appear anywhere and take today's cash.

## Crew voices (first-person testimony)

**Khurshid Alam, driver, Reuters 2018.** Drives 6 am to 11 pm, three days a week, up to Tk 1,200 a day. Reported his cracked windscreen to the owner; never replaced. "If we get a monthly salary we can drive without any tension. Everybody has a guardian to take care of their interests but we don't." A rickshaw cut in front mid-interview and he hard-braked.

**Nur Islam, Khajababa Paribahan driver, Bangla Tribune 2023.** Rule is one day driving, one day rest, so one day's income covers two days. Even overloading, he clears Tk 1,200–1,500 a day, Tk 1,800 on a good one. Hands the owner's deposit over at night; what's left is his and the helper's.

**Abbas, Bihongo bus driver, same piece.** Wants owners to say "drivers just drive, don't think about income"; the pressure of the owner's deposit and his own earnings never lets up. No fixed hours: work until enough trips are done.

**Khalek Mia, Projapoti Paribahan driver, Prothom Alo 2018.** Under the deposit system, after the owner's money, gas and "line costs" he and his helper got whatever remained. Owners' association said monthly pay is impossible because no driver stays with one owner for a month, so a per-trip daily wage was set instead.

**Raida bus driver, 2025.** Before, after deposit, fuel and food, Tk 3,000–5,000 was left; now under the new wage system less than Tk 1,000 reaches him at the end of the day. An owner, Shahjalal: "We are owners in name; the real owners are the drivers and helpers. They come at day's end and pay *us*."

**State minister for roads, 28 Sep 2026.** Confirms the daily deposit is Tk 3,000–5,000 depending on route; drivers of the *same company* race each other because the deposit can't be met otherwise; companies also collect Tk 300–500 per bus per day with no transparent accounting, allegedly shared with officials, political leaders, local strongmen and thugs; joining a company costs Tk 500 to Tk 100,000 per bus.

**Hare Krishnow, conductor, NPR 2024.** Leans out of a red double-decker banging the side for attention, shouting destinations; the bus doesn't fully stop; fare about 8 US cents.

**Helpers in the news, 2025–2026.** Dulal (25), helper on an Ahsan Paribahan bus, died on the Dhaka–Mawa expressway after brakes failed: he jumped out of the moving bus and tried to wedge wood and heavy objects under the rear wheel; the wheel ran over him; 40 passengers survived. Family in Kurigram depended entirely on his income; his wife needs Tk 10,000 a month for treatment. Nayeem Howlader (30), helper on a Unique bus, fell from the open door at Sayedabad Janapath and died. Ariful Islam Babu, Sraban Transport helper, described a plainclothes officer beating him and the driver before seizing the bus. A university bus driver described two men beating his helper after an argument with a motorcyclist.

**Recruitment.** A driver whose daughter was killed in 2018: owners hire on relationships and recommendations without testing skill; most climb from windscreen wiper to helper to "five-star ostad" and never learn to drive properly.

## Traffic and character AI (method)

No machine learning. Hand-authored logic with tunable numbers; personality is the numbers.

**Two layers, kept separate.** *Steering* decides how a vehicle moves; *decision* decides what it wants. A bug in one never breaks the other.

**Steering (all vehicles)**

- Lane-free corridors: centreline + width, free lateral offset, no lanes
- Steering behaviours: follow corridor, seek gap, speed-scaled minimum distance
- Yield by mass and nerve: mass (bus 20, truck 15, car 3, CNG 1.5, rickshaw 1, pedestrian 0.3); smaller yields unless nerve calls the bluff
- Critical gap 0.5–1.5 s scaled by nerve (Western sims use 4–6 s)
- Horn: broadcast in a cone with strength; raises yield probability of agents inside, weighted by relative mass; silence moves nothing
- Junctions: an officer object opens one direction at a time with variable timing; a few agents "leak" across until blocked
- Pedestrians: cross on estimated gap; "hand" confidence makes them step out in front of small vehicles and hesitate for buses
- Performance: full behaviours within 100–150 m of player; kinematic corridor-following beyond; GPU-instanced crowd animation

**Decision: utility AI for buses**

Every second, score each action; do the highest. Actions and what raises their score:

| Action | Score rises when |
| --- | --- |
| Race for next stop | big crowd ahead, bus half empty, rival close behind |
| Skip the stop | nearly full, small crowd |
| Wait and fill | early in route, no rival near |
| Block the player | player about to overtake, driver's spite high |
| Back off | fatigue high, dangerous gap ahead |

Each driver has personal weights per action (reckless: race ×1.5, back off ×0.5; cautious: reverse; spiteful: block ×2 plus memory of who cut him off).

**Memory and relationships (per character)**

Variables: grudge toward player, trust, fatigue, money today. Events move them (player cuts him off: grudge +1; lets him through: grudge −1). They feed the utility weights and select terminal dialogue (grudge > 3 picks cold lines; a shared bad day picks tired, friendly ones).

**Two tiers of buses**

- *Own company (2 buses):* persistent named crews, same livery as the player's, full utility AI with memory, present at terminal scenes, part of the story cast. They compete hardest (shared deposit, shared passengers), as the minister described.
- *Other companies:* generic liveries, no memory, simple race-when-near logic, spawned and despawned like traffic.

**Passengers as demand zones**

Density curves along the road by time of day (residential origins in the morning, Farmgate/Motijheel in the evening). Each passenger boards the first bus unless it's too full. Per-passenger boarding time: student fast, elderly with sack slow, half-fare arguer costs conductor attention.

**Tuning**

One table for every number (mass, nerve, critical gap, horn strength, officer timing, action weights). Playtest metrics: follow distance, near misses per minute, stops lost, horn presses. "Feels random" means nerve too high or gaps too small; "polite driving still wins" means rivals too timid.

**Dialogue**

Scripted Bangla lines with subtitles, chosen by state. No generative AI needed. Unity ML-Agents only as an optional later tool for tuning gap thresholds.

**Scale:** 2,000–4,000 lines of C#; steering items 1–4 give most of the feel and come first.
