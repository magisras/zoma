## Why
The owner asked what is specific about a Dhaka bus and what to look for in a model. These are the reference
for proportions, the body style and the livery until photographs of the chosen bus exist.

## Facts
- **S-026.1** The full-size city bus is a locally built body on a truck chassis: flat front, two-piece windscreen, high waistline, sliding windows in a steel frame, a single passenger door on the left behind the front axle, roof hatches, bumper rail. Rust along every panel seam; the livery repainted (photo 00, Bikash Paribahan, a Tata-type body).
- **S-026.2** The helper rides in the open left door, one hand on the rail, calling the stops; the door itself is gone (photo 05, Midway).
- **S-026.3** The route is painted along the side in Bangla as a list of stops, with the route number: e.g. ‘… Kalabagan, Asad Gate, Shyamoli, Gabtoli, route no. 7/145’ (photo 03).
- **S-026.4** Two sizes share the kerb: the full-size 52-seater and the shorter Isuzu/Toyota minibus with a roof rack and a spare tyre on it (photo 01, Jabale Noor and minibuses at a stop).
- **S-026.5** Inside: a dark padded ceiling, roof hatches, fluorescent tubes, patterned seat covers, the conductor walking the aisle collecting fares, people standing from a yellow handrail (photos 04, 06).
- **S-026.6** Dents and patches on every corner; minibuses in green, orange and white with the company name and a logo (photo 09).

- **S-026.7** The current generation of the same bus (owner's photo, 9 Oct 2026, origin not recorded, reference only): a
  Bikash Paribahan body in magenta and blue, one-piece curved windscreen, a copied Mercedes star on the grille, LED
  lamps, roof dome, six windows a side, the doorway behind the front axle with no door, dual rear wheels. The body is
  by a local coachbuilder (the name on the window glass reads TAMIM) on the same kind of truck chassis as the older,
  flat-fronted body in photo 00. Same company, two generations of body, both on the road.
