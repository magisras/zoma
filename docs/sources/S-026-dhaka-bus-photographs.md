## Why
The owner asked what is specific about a Dhaka bus and what to look for in a model. These are the reference
for proportions, the body style and the livery until photographs of the chosen bus exist.

## Facts
- **S-026.1** The full-size city bus is a locally built body on a truck chassis: flat front, two-piece windscreen, high waistline, sliding windows in a steel frame, a single passenger door on the left behind the front axle, roof hatches, bumper rail. Rust along every panel seam; the livery repainted (photo 00, Bikash Paribahan, a Tata-type body).
- **S-026.2** The helper rides in the open left door, one hand on the rail, calling the stops; the door itself is gone (photo 05, Midway).
- **S-026.3** The route is painted along the side in Bangla as a list of stops, with the route number: e.g. ‘… Kalabagan, Asad Gate, Shyamoli, Gabtoli, route no. 7/145’ (photo 03).
- **S-026.4** Two sizes share the kerb: the full-size 52-seater and the shorter Isuzu/Toyota minibus with a roof rack and a spare tyre on it (photo 01, Jabale Noor and minibuses at a stop).
- **S-026.5** Inside: a dark padded ceiling, roof hatches, fluorescent tubes, patterned seat covers, the conductor walking the aisle collecting fares, people standing from a yellow handrail (photos 04, 06).
- **S-026.6** Dents and patches on every corner; minibuses in green, orange and white with the company name and a logo (photo 09).

- **S-026.7** The current generation of the same bus (owner's photo, 9 Oct 2026, origin not recorded, reference only): a
  Bikash Paribahan body in magenta and blue, one-piece curved windscreen, a copied Mercedes star on the grille, LED
  lamps, roof dome, six windows a side, the doorway behind the front axle with no door, dual rear wheels. The body is
  by a local coachbuilder (the name on the window glass reads TAMIM) on the same kind of truck chassis as the older,
  flat-fronted body in photo 00. Same company, two generations of body, both on the road.

- **S-026.8** The driver's cab from the seat (owner-supplied frame, 10 Oct 2026, from Andrew Fraser's "Why Bangladesh Has the Deadliest Buses on Earth", RESEARCH.md; reference only):
  right-hand drive; every surface painted orange-red metal; a one-piece windscreen in a thick frame with a painted
  name band across its top in Bangla and a cloth curtain hanging at the top left; a large rectangular inside mirror
  on a stalk from the top of the screen, right of centre; a black car-type steering wheel, small, lying well back,
  low and close to the driver; a raised instrument binnacle in front of it under a red cloth; a paper sheet taped to
  the dash left of the wheel (the waybill or a fare chart); an open cubby with cloth in it further left; the engine
  cowl between the driver and the door under a cloth; bare metal floor; the right side window open with the outside
  mirror's arm at its edge; a passenger stepping in at the open door on the left. The bus ahead has a hand-painted
  rear with a number plate on a board. The cab in `BusCabin` is laid out from this frame.
- **S-026.9** The dash close up (owner-supplied frame, 10 Oct 2026, the same Fraser film, reference only): a white panel
  taped to the dash left of the binnacle carrying four rows of six small switches, two rows of toggles and two of
  round push buttons, the bus's own wiring (lights, horns, wipers); the instrument binnacle's hood with a checked
  gamcha cloth stuffed over the dials; a long black rod along the base of the screen; the windscreen cracked in a
  star; strips of tape across the dash; an open cubby on the left with a water bottle and cloth; the inside mirror's
  stalk wrapped in black tape.
- **S-026.10** The cab looking left from the driver's seat (owner-supplied frame, 10 Oct 2026, same source,
  reference only): a padded bench in red floral vinyl runs along the left wall of the cab under the sliding side
  windows, from the front corner back towards the door, facing inward across the engine cowl, so passengers sit
  beside the driver (the owner: "to fit more customers inside"); the cubby with the bottle and the switch panel
  are on the dash's left end; stickers and a phone number on the name band; the inside mirror top right shows the
  passengers behind.
- **S-026.11** The saloon looking forward from the back (owner-supplied frame, 10 Oct 2026, the same Fraser film,
  reference only): two-and-two high-backed seats, each back in a red floral vinyl cover tied on, green cushions,
  a narrow aisle of bare dirty metal, luggage racks of slatted metal along both sides overhead, a ceiling of metal
  slats with fans, stickers on the sliding windows, and the cab ahead with the painted name band across the top of
  the screen and the orange dash. The interior in `BusInterior` is laid out from this frame.
